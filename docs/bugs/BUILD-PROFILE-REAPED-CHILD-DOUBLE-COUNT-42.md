## Status: 17 October 2026

Fixed on the v0.0.42 line (commit f7b6108, gate 2661; merged into the line as 20de470, gate 2558).

## Symptom

`tests/test_build_profile.py` `test_cpu_and_wall_of_a_stage_and_all_its_children` failed on loaded
hosts while it passed alone: the sampled timeline of a stage summed more CPU than the stage really
used (its own `getrusage`). Gate 2519 (16 October): 3.59 s against a bound of 2.07 s; gate 2610:
2.14 s against 2.12 s; gate 2651: 2.19 s against 2.18 s; gate 2556 (17 October, on the v0.0.42 line):
2.67 s against 2.15 s.

The same overcount went into every build profile: the sampled build-machine threads used per
stage were reported too high for the sample after a child process of the stage ended.

## Where

`tools/build_profile.py`, the sampler thread (`Sampler.sample`): the per-stage CPU of each sample.

## How it happened

Each sample reads the stage's process tree: every live process's own CPU, plus the CPU of the
children each process has already waited for. When a child ended between the walk's reads, or had
ended and was not yet reaped by its parent, that sample saw less CPU than the one before (the dip).
The sampler took the dip as its new baseline and reported 0 threads for that sample. When the
parent then reaped the child, the child's whole CPU showed up in the parent's reaped-children
total and was counted a second time. On a loaded host the gap between those reads is longer, so
the dip happens more often.

## Why it was not caught

TEST-PROFILE-TIMELINE-BOUND-33 (9 October, v0.0.33-dev) saw the same test fail by a few
milliseconds. It was read as sample rounding and the bound was widened from 0.1 s to 0.2 s. The
cause was not traced, so the problem PERSISTED: it came back as the larger overcounts above.

## Reproduction

Sometimes: run the profiler tests on a heavily loaded host. Always:
`test_timeline_counts_a_reaped_child_once` replays the readings 0.0, 1.0, 0.2 (dip), 1.2, 1.5 s.
The old sampler summed 2.3 s for a tree that used 1.5 s.

## Repair

The sampler's baseline never goes down. A dip reports 0 threads and keeps the higher earlier
reading, so the child's CPU is counted once, when it ran. The timeline is again a lower bound of
the real CPU. The test's assertions are unchanged (the timeline stays within 0.2 s of the measured
CPU). `tools/build_profile.py` is output-neutral and is in no stage key.

## Verification

The new deterministic test passes. The old sampler fails it (2.3 s instead of 1.5 s). Gates 2661
and 2558 passed with the full suite on loaded hosts.

## Which recorded numbers are affected

- Not affected: each stage's CPU seconds and average build-machine threads used (`cpu`,
  `cores_avg` in `build-profile.json`). These come from the stage's own `getrusage`, not from the
  sampler. The perf guard (`tools/perf_guard.py`) judges section `cores_avg` / `cpu`, so its
  baselines do not need re-recording for this bug. The cgroup series of the Build Timeline
  (`timeline.cgroup_cores`, used when present: every Docker build) comes from the container's
  cgroup counter, so it is not affected either.
- Possibly too high: the sampled per-stage series (`timeline.stages`) and the summed sampled
  series (`timeline.cores`, used by the Build Timeline when no cgroup series exists). Also what is
  read from them: the per-section threads in the Monitor, `idle_periods` with the timeline source,
  and the build's own share in the host-load split. The error shows as short spikes after a child
  ends. It inflates highs, never lows. So peak readings such as dev5's "20-24 threads" in the
  v0.0.42 fixlist (B1) may be a little high; the drops to 4-8 threads are real. The share of time
  under 12 threads (B5) may be slightly understated. The 17.7 average of B5 is not affected if it
  is a stage's `cores_avg`.
- Not checked here: section phase timers take CPU as two whole-tree readings (start, end). A dip
  at the start reading could make one phase's CPU too high in the same way. This is rare: the
  children of earlier phases are normally reaped by then.

## Prevention

The deterministic replay test. Any sampler change must keep the timeline a lower bound of the
measured CPU.
