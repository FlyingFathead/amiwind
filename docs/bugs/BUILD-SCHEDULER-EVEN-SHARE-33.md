## Status: 9 October 2026

Open. Proposed repair below; not changed yet.

## Symptom

A MiniWind build with a budget of 4 workers ran its media stage (4,875 sounds to convert, the
longest stage of the build) on 1 worker for its first 92 s, 2 until 302 s, 3 for a moment, 2 until
492 s and 1 after that, while the stages beside it (engine, intro, census, harvest, hand-catalog,
chim) each held the same share. Its worker changes in the build state:
`[[92.5, 2], [302.6, 3], [302.6, 2], [492.7, 1]]`.

## Where

`tools/build_parallel.py`, `execute_parallel` (`rebalance`).

## How it happened

`rebalance` gives serial stages one worker each, keeps one worker for each ready stage still
waiting, and splits the rest evenly between the running pooled stages, ordered by start time. It
does not know how much work each stage has left.

## Why it was not caught

On the usual 22-worker budget an even share is still several workers per stage; the effect shows
with small budgets, which background and MiniWind builds use.

## Reproduction

A build with `--jobs 4` whose media stage runs at the same time as other pooled stages; read the
media stage's `worker_changes` in `build-state.json`.

## Repair

Proposed: weight each running pooled stage's share by its expected remaining work, taken from the
stage's processor time in the `--reuse-from` run's profile (or the item cost history,
`tools/build_costs.py`), never below one worker; an even split when no history exists. Outputs do
not depend on worker counts, so the change is checked by the scheduler tests and the profile of the
next build.

## Verification

Planned: a scheduler test with stages of different recorded cost, and a MiniWind build on 4 workers
compared with this one.

## Prevention

The builder profile (docs/performance/BUILDER_PROFILE.md) lists stages that held fewer workers than
their remaining work needed.
