## Status: 19 October 2026

Reported by the maintainer; a measured improvement is on a branch, not yet in a release.

20 October 2026: the type 1 prototype (one window of region frames, no level change at their borders; setting
`chim crossing 1`, off by default) was measured on the crossing route: the worst frame after a light-area crossing
fell from 83 to 42 ms with 128 MB and from 373 to 66 ms with 16 MB; the work before the border at 16 MB grew (the
approach's worst frame 66 to 80 ms). Crossings into and out of towns stay level changes.

Corrected 20 October 2026: those figures are not a like-for-like comparison. Type 0's crossing creates the new
frame's entities (people, creatures, doors, harvestable plants and the models they load); the type 1 prototype
creates none in the frames it joins, so the figures do not show how fast a complete crossing would be. The
prototype is not the default and will not be until it carries entities. Details, limits and the plan:
[Cell crossings](../chim/CELL_CROSSINGS.md#the-type-1-prototype-measured-20-october-2026).

## Symptom

Walking from one CHIM cell into the next stalls the game for a moment. It is worst at cell corners, where four
frames meet.

## Where

Every CHIM cell crossing in the open world.

## How it happened

Each crossing loads the next frame's chunks from disk while the player waits. At a corner, more frames change at
once.

## Why it was not caught

Crossings were measured on straight walks between two frames, not at corners.

## Reproduction

Always: walk across a cell border, and across a cell corner.

## Repair

Decided for the project, 19 October 2026: AmiWind uses the RAM an accelerator card has. 16 MB stays the baseline
that must work; memory past it goes to the game, first as the frame cache below and a larger game heap
([ENGINE-HEAP-FIXED-SIZE-43](ENGINE-HEAP-FIXED-SIZE-43.md): today a fixed 11 MiB even with 128 MB).

Planned for v0.0.45-dev1, in this order:

1. Use the spare RAM as a frame cache: with more than 16 MB, every neighbouring frame stays resident, and frames
   the player just left stay until the memory is needed, so a crossing is a copy in memory instead of a disk read.
   The prefetch on branch `v0.0.43-prefetch` does the first part (measured: median crossing about 450 ms down to
   about 230 ms with 128 MB of RAM).
   The cache adapts to the memory there is (maintainer, 19 October 2026): with 16 MB the game keeps what fits and
   loads the rest at the border, as today; with 128 MB, 1 GiB or more it keeps proportionally more frames ready,
   up to every frame the player can reach next. One build for all, sized at start from the free memory.
2. Spread the loading over many frames: read a small piece (a chunk or a model) per rendered frame while the
   player walks through the band between the load radius and the active ring, instead of one blocking read at the
   border.
3. Corners: inside a corner's band, queue all three other frames, the one ahead in the direction of travel first.
4. Load earlier than unload (hysteresis), so walking along a border or around a corner never loads and drops the
   same frames again.

Even with the frames in RAM, about 230 ms per crossing is left: CPU work at the switch, not disk. Before more
changes, a crossing is timed section by section (read, parse, link placements, recentre, surface cache rebuild) at
an edge and at a corner. The options, chosen by those numbers:

- Frames stored ready to use: the `.chim` frame laid out as it sits in memory, pointers as offsets; loading is a
  copy and a few fix-ups, with no parsing.
- The next frame prepared before the border: parsed, models registered and placements linked a slice per rendered
  frame, so the crossing only switches the active frame.
- No whole-frame switch: chunks come and go one by one as they enter and leave the ring around the player.
- The surface cache kept across the switch.

Today's crossings carry almost no NPCs: most of the open world has no NPC placements yet (they come after the
interiors and the object state, [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#the-road-after-v0044-19-october-2026)). The streaming is
designed and measured for the load they will add: each frame's actors, their models and their state are part of
what is prepared before the border, and the measurements include a heavy town frame with its people in place.

## Verification

Every option is an A/B/C run, not a guess: the same scripted route (edge and corner crossings) on the same image
settings, at 16 MB and at 128 MB, with the profiler's per-section crossing times and frame-time log for each
variant (A the current method, B and on one change each, A again as a drift check). The optimizer keeps a change
only when its numbers beat A; every result goes into this page.

## Prevention
