## Status: 10 October 2026

Open; repaired in source behind two experimental build options (off by default): `--chim-terrain-hull compiled`
and `--chim-terrain-refs on`. With both, the region frames of two scratch worlds take 0.91 and 0.93 times the
bytes of the open-world maps they replace. Closed once a sea-ring build with both options has been played.

## Symptom

A CHIM region frame ([CHIM_REGIONS.md](../chim/CHIM_REGIONS.md)) replaces one cell's open-world map. Its
ground reaches 896 units past the cell on every side (terrain only), so the frame is 15 x 15 chunks for a
cell of 8 x 8: about 3.5 times the cell's terrain. Three neighbouring cells (one coastal land cell, two seabed
cells) took 3,459,228 bytes of CHIM frame files (854,836 to 1,493,064 per frame, far layers 23,934 bytes)
where their legacy open-world maps took 676,296 bytes. Extrapolated to the 172 cells of the sea ring (70.2 MB
of legacy maps), the image would grow by about 120 MB.

## Where

`tools/chim/regions.py` `TERRAIN_MARGIN` (896) and one frame per cell; the margin rule itself is the
stitcher's ([CHIM-FAR-BAND-GAP-36](CHIM-FAR-BAND-GAP-36.md)): ground up to the fog plane from every
hand-over point, at least draw distance + 32 = 572 units. 896 is the smallest margin of whole 256-unit chunks
that the world's sector size 3 divides (8 + 2 x 3.5 = 15 chunks).

## How it happened

One cell per frame keeps the engine's open-world hand-over and every open-world tool unchanged (the frame
map takes the cell's map name). The price is a margin on every side of every cell, also between two CHIM
cells, where each neighbour stores its own copy of the shared band.

## Why it was not caught

The CHIMport per-cell figures (about 290 KB for a seabed cell) were measured without a margin; the cost
appears only once the margin rule applies to cell frames.

## Reproduction

Always: any `--chim-regions` build.

## Repair

Two parts, each a build option, off by default (the copies and the routed hull stay the default methods):

- The bulk of each chunk was its standing hull: routed chains of the expanded tile pieces (2,275 of 5,640 bytes
  per chunk on average). `--chim-terrain-hull compiled` compiles the same pieces into one exact BSP with qbsp,
  the hull Seyda Neen's irregular ground already uses: about half the bytes.
- Terrain references (world format 0.7, [WORLD_FORMAT.md](../chim/WORLD_FORMAT.md) "Terrain references"):
  `--chim-terrain-refs on` builds every chunk's terrain in chunk-local units and stores it once per content; a
  chunk names the record and the offset it is placed at, and the engine moves its copy when it grafts the chunk
  into the frame world. Neighbouring frames share their margins, and repeated sea floor is one record.

Frames of several cells were measured not to be needed: on the one-cell-wide sea ring, references already store
the union of the frames' grounds once.

## Verification

Scratch CHIM worlds (one frame per cell, 896-unit margin) against the open-world maps of the same cells:

| Cells | Open-world maps | Copies, routed hull | Copies, compiled hull | References, routed hull | References, compiled hull |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 (3 coast, 4 sea floor) | 2,105,504 | 9,012,728 | 4,908,676 | 4,253,128 | 1,912,960 |
| 6 (coast) | 2,768,088 | 10,024,380 | 5,089,260 | 6,022,992 | 2,586,432 |

Validation, the stair gate, the CHIM heap gate and the zone walk passed on both reference worlds; file counts
are unchanged. Tests: `tests/test_chim_terrain_refs.py` (moving a chunk-local unit equals building it in place;
a world with references reads as the world with copies; the shift rule) and the engine's native tests (the
whole ring scenario and the chunk-border standing test on 0.7 worlds; 0.7 read, 0.8 refused). To come: the image
size of the first sea-ring playtest built with both options.

## Prevention

The receipt of every `--chim-regions` image records the bytes of the replaced legacy maps and of the frame
maps; the CHIM world's stats record the frame bytes.
