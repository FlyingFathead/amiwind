# NPC pathfinding: how NPCs find their way

Investigation, 9 October 2026 (owner request). How Morrowind, OpenMW and Quake
move their characters around, what AmiWind does today, what the options cost
on a 68040, and a staged plan. The CHIM side (where the walkable graph lives
in the world format, budgets and gates) is in
[CHIM NPC pathfinding](chim/CHIM_NPC_PATHFINDING.md).

Status: design and measurement. No engine or QuakeC change has been made.
Every number below is an aggregate measured from the owner's own master files
or from builder output; no record is copied. Emulator and cycle figures are
relative or estimated, as stated at each one.

## Summary

- **Morrowind stores a small graph per cell, not a mesh.** Each cell may carry
  one path grid (record `PGRD`): points with positions and links between them.
  On Vvardenfell (`Morrowind.esm`) 1,194 of 2,538 cells have one: 1,060 of 1,134
  interiors (93 %) but only 134 of 1,404 exteriors (10 %). The island holds
  41,745 points and 54,888 links; a grid has 24 points at the median and 246
  at most. Grids never link to the next cell.
- **Most outdoor actors stand where there is no grid.** 4,597 of the 5,082
  exterior levelled-creature spawn points, and 492 placed exterior actors, are
  in cells without a path grid. Any outdoor hostile needs a method that works
  without Morrowind's data.
- **OpenMW** builds a navigation mesh at run time with Recast/Detour from the
  collision shapes, joins the loaded cells, adds the path grid links into it,
  and falls back to an A\* over the cell's grid only when the mesh fails.
- **Quake** has no map of the level at all: a monster turns toward its enemy,
  tries a step, and picks another of eight directions when blocked. Cheap,
  stairs included, but walls and dead ends lose it.
- **AmiWind today:** only the two scripted opening guards walk (ship escort and
  dock guard), with a breadth-first route over the original path grid and the
  player's own step code. Every other NPC stands, idles, turns and greets.
- **Recommendation (the ladder):** (1) Quake-style chase on the player's step
  code, which already climbs every flight the stair gate passes; (2) a stuck
  check with a short local probe; (3) path-grid routing on a small stored
  walkable graph, only when the target is out of sight or the NPC is stuck,
  with a fixed node budget per frame. No navmesh. The stored graph costs about
  0.1 MB for the island's exterior grids plus an estimated 0.6 MB at most for
  generated graphs in the cells Morrowind left without one.

## How Morrowind does it

### The path grid record

A path grid belongs to one cell (interior by name, exterior by grid
coordinates) and holds, little-endian:

| Subrecord | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `NAME` | cell name (an exterior grid may carry the cell's or the region's name) |
| `DATA` (12 bytes) | i32 grid x, i32 grid y (0, 0 for interiors), u16 granularity, u16 point count |
| `PGRP` (16 bytes per point) | i32 x, y, z (exterior points relative to the cell's corner), u8 flags (1 = generated by the editor), u8 link count, 2 bytes padding |
| `PGRC` (4 bytes per link) | target point index for each link, the points' links in point order |

Public format references: the OpenMW loader
([`components/esm3/loadpgrd.hpp`](https://gitlab.com/OpenMW/openmw/-/blob/master/components/esm3/loadpgrd.hpp))
and the [UESP file format page](https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind_Mod:Mod_File_Format/PGRD).
Our measurement confirms the layout on the owner's data: every grid's point
count matches its `PGRP` size, 133 of the 134 exterior grids lie inside
their cell's 8,192-unit square, and every link is stored both ways (0 one-way
links island-wide). Granularity is the editor's sampling step when it
generates a grid (1,024 units for 737 grids, 256 for 265); 89 % of all points
carry the "generated" flag, so most grids came out of the editor's generator
and were then edited by hand.

### How the game uses it

Each actor carries an ordered list of behaviour packages (subrecords `AI_W` wander,
`AI_T` travel, `AI_F` follow, `AI_E` escort, `AI_A` activate) plus its behaviour
settings (`AIDT`: hello, fight, flee, alarm). Wander gives a distance, a
duration, an hour and eight idle weights; the actor picks destinations among
the cell's grid points within that distance of where it started, walks there
and plays idles. Travel, escort and follow move toward a point or an actor.
Combat chases the target.

What is certain from the data:

- The graph is per cell and has no links across cell borders. On Vvardenfell
  754 exterior points lie within 256 units of a cell edge, 596 of them facing a
  cell that has its own grid; nothing joins them.
- A cell without a grid gives an actor nothing to route on.

How the original engine searched the grid and handled cell borders is thinly
documented in public sources. OpenMW's reimplementation (below) is the best
public reference: an A\* over the current cell's grid, with straight lines
where there is no grid. Players know the visible result: actors stuck on rocks
and corners, unable to reach a player standing on a ledge or a rock.

## How OpenMW does it

OpenMW 0.51.0 (source read from the release tree; paths relative to the
[OpenMW repository](https://gitlab.com/OpenMW/openmw)):

- **Navigation mesh, built at run time** (`components/detournavigator/`, the
  recastnavigation library). The input is the physics collision of objects,
  the terrain heightfield of each loaded exterior cell, water, doors as
  two-way off-mesh links and every path grid link as an off-mesh link. The
  mesh is cut into tiles (128 voxels of 0.2 x 34 units, about 870 units a tile)
  around the player, about 17 tiles in radius (`maxTilesNumber` 1,024). Actor
  limits match the player: climb 34 units, slope 46 degrees. Tiles are built
  on a background thread and cached in memory (256 MiB cap by default) and on
  disk (`navmesh.db`, 2 GiB cap). Settings: `[Navigator]` in
  `files/settings-default.cfg`, documented in
  `docs/source/reference/modding/settings/navigator.rst`.
- **Path search** (`apps/openmw/mwmechanics/pathfinding.cpp`): a Detour
  polygon path with string pulling first; if that fails, again with the grid
  links allowed; then an A\* over the current cell's grid (Manhattan
  heuristic, start and end at the nearest connected points, connected parts
  precomputed per cell); then a straight line.
- **Wander** (`aiwander.cpp`): destinations are the cell's grid points within
  the wander distance that connect to the nearest point; with too few points,
  a random navmesh point near the start. It avoids water and occupied spots.
- **Stuck handling** (`obstacle.cpp`): an actor that moved less than half its
  expected distance over 1.5 s is stuck and tries up to seven evasion
  directions, one second each; next to a usable door it opens it. The actor
  reacts every 0.25 s; a direct shortcut is tried when the target is in sight.
- **Cell borders:** grids are never linked across cells; outdoors the navmesh
  joins the loaded cells, and actors near the edge of the loaded area pause.

The design point for us: OpenMW needs the navmesh because the grids alone
give the clumsy original behaviour. Its costs (a background builder thread,
hundreds of megabytes of tile cache, Detour queries per actor) are far beyond
an Amiga; the idea we keep is "grid links plus a fallback".

## How Quake does it

AmiQuake (engine/aga/src) keeps id's monster movement unchanged apart from
the step height:

- `SV_movestep` (`sv_move.c`): move the box, lift it by `STEPSIZE`, trace it
  down `2 x STEPSIZE` with the entity's hull, refuse starting in solid or
  stepping off a drop, then `SV_CheckBottom` tests the four bottom corners
  (no corner may hang more than `STEPSIZE` below the centre). AmiWind set
  `STEPSIZE` to 8.5 units (34 Morrowind units), the player's step.
- `SV_StepDirection`, `SV_NewChaseDir`, `SV_MoveToGoal` (QuakeC builtin 67,
  `movetogoal`) and `PF_walkmove` (builtin 32): turn toward the goal, step;
  when blocked, try the diagonal toward the enemy, then the two axis
  directions toward it, then the old direction, then all eight 45-degree
  directions in a random sense, and only then turn around. One chance in four
  per step to re-pick even when not blocked. A blocked monster can thus try
  up to about eleven steps in one think.
- id's QuakeC (not part of AmiWind's progs) drives this from `ai_walk` /
  `ai_run` every 0.1 s; patrols follow chains of `path_corner` entities (each
  names the next; touching one sets the next goal).

Strengths: no data, no search, stairs and slopes handled by the same step
trace as the player. Weaknesses: greedy; a wall between monster and player,
a U-shaped dead end or a door off to the side defeats it. Cost: one step is
about six hull traces (measured below on a CHIM frame), so a fully blocked
think can cost some sixty.

## What AmiWind does today

No wander, patrol, follow or combat movement exists. Two actors move, both in
the opening, both native C (not QuakeC):

- **Ship escort** (`aw_intro.c`, the "original name/escort script adapter"):
  `AW_IntroTick` steps a state machine that sends intro role 2 to goals taken
  from the original CharGen scripts' travel targets.
- **Dock guard** (`aw_opening.c`): role 5 walks a fixed route and stops when
  the player comes close, as the original wander package does.
- **Routing** (`aw_nav.c`): `AW_NavStart` finds the grid points nearest the
  actor and the goal and runs a breadth-first search; `AW_NavStep` walks at 22
  units/s, cuts corners when a hull trace is clear, steers ±30/60/90 degrees
  around blocks, waits for the player, and gives up after 5 s blocked or 8 s
  without progress. One walker at a time, at most 128 points and 512 links.
- **Grids:** the builder converts exactly two path grids, the prison ship's
  (`tools/prepare_intro.py`, `id1/intro/prison.awn`) and Seyda Neen's exterior
  grids (`tools/prepare_character.py`, `id1/intro/seyda.awn`, two grids of 95
  and 11 points, no links invented between cells).
- **Step code** (`aw_walk.c`): `AW_ActorStep` moves an actor with the
  player's walk (`AW_WalkPlayer`, around `SV_WalkMove`): step up 8.5 units,
  floor normal at least `AW_WALKABLE_Z` 0.69 (46 degrees), no step off a
  ledge. Its comment records why: Quake's corner test refused parts of the
  ship the player can walk.
- **Everyone else** (`engine/aga/qc/world.qc`): `aw_npc` is `MOVETYPE_NONE`,
  snapped to the ground once (`AW_NPCFloor`, builtin 80, using the builder's
  baked ground fit from `tools/actor_grounding.py`), then idles, turns toward
  the player and greets. The progs declare neither `walkmove` nor
  `movetogoal`, although the engine still has both builtins.
- **Packages:** `src/mwad/npc.py` decodes `AIDT` and the wander package
  (distance, duration, hour, idles) and keeps the others in order; nothing
  reaches the maps yet (`runtime_wandering` is always false).

See also the [NPC cell traversal requirement](NPC_CELL_TRAVERSAL.md) and
[roadmap](ROADMAP.md) (planned NPC steps: walk poses, path-grid routes inside
the wander radius, suspension while greeting).

## Grid on CHIM collision (measured)

Morrowind's grid points and links were placed for Morrowind's collision.
Before trusting them on ours, a private probe put every exterior point and
link of two CHIM worlds (format 0.5, builder output) onto the frame's
standing-hull collision, with the same offline tracer the stair gate uses
(`chim.collision.FrameScene`). Each link was walked in 8-unit steps twice:
with the player-style step that `AW_ActorStep` uses, and with Quake's
`SV_movestep` (corner test included).

| Frame | Points on walkable collision | Points inside solid | Links walked, player step | Links walked, `SV_movestep` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Balmora | 393 of 395 (22 more than 8 units off the grid height) | 2 | 642 of 662 (97 %) | 610 of 662 (92 %) |
| Seyda Neen | 104 of 106 (3 off, 1 of them on a steep face) | 2 | 144 of 156 (92 %) | 134 of 156 (86 %) |

(3 links in each frame have an end inside solid and were not walked.) So the
player step walks more of the original routes than Quake's monster step,
confirming the comment in `aw_walk.c`; and a few percent of the links need a
builder repair (a moved point or an inserted one). Each probe ran in under a
minute on one core.

Trace work per step (offline tracer, clipnode visits counted; the engine's
`SV_RecursiveHullCheck` does comparable work, but these are counts, not
cycles):

| Frame | Player step: traces, visits | `SV_movestep`: traces, visits | 16-way fan of 32-unit traces: visits per trace, median / p90 / mean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Balmora | 2.6, 250 | 6.0, 685 | 48 / 179 / 544 |
| Seyda Neen | 3.1, 1,022 | 6.0, 968 | 171 / 3,040 / 2,003 |

The means are dominated by a tail of traces that graze many planes (the same
fan lifted by one step was not cheaper). The tail is registered as
[CHIM-TRACE-TAIL-33](bugs/CHIM-TRACE-TAIL-33.md).

## Options for AmiWind on the Amiga

Budget reality: Balmora on CHIM draws about one frame per second on a 50 MHz
68040 (cycle-exact emulation, relative). Navigation must be a small fraction
of that, spread over frames, and only for actors near the player. Cycle
figures below are estimates (about 80 cycles per clipnode visit, 500 per
trace set-up, 400 per placed hull entered; to be replaced by an engine
counter on the cycle-exact preset, stage 1 below).

| Option | What | Memory (island) | CPU per use (50 MHz 040, estimate) | Covers gridless cells | Fits CHIM streaming |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Morrowind grids as compact node graphs stored once per CHIM sector, search on demand | exterior grids about 0.1 MB on disk, interiors about 0.5 MB with their maps; a few KB resident | A\* over a 256-unit patch: 0.1 ms median, 0.9 ms worst | no (10 % of exterior cells have a grid) | yes: streams with its sector |
| B | Quake chase (greedy steps, re-pick on block) plus `path_corner`-style routes for scripted walks | none | one step 0.5 ms (Balmora) to 1.8 ms (Seyda Neen) | yes | nothing to stream |
| C | Baked navmesh polygons (as OpenMW) built offline by the builder | estimate tens of MB island-wide (polygons, links, detail heights per tile); Recast as a new builder dependency | polygon search plus string pulling: several times A\* over a graph | yes | yes, but large |
| D | Hybrid (the ladder): B always; a stuck check with a short probe; A on a graph that the builder also generates where Morrowind has no grid | A plus at most about 0.6 MB generated (estimate) | B per step; probe 1.7-15 ms (Balmora); A\* as A | yes | yes |

Details:

- **A, graphs.** 7 bytes per point plus 1-2 per link end today (the CHIM
  layout uses 8 and 2, see the CHIM page). A\* with integer coordinates and a
  small binary heap is estimated at about 400 cycles per expanded point; the
  1,024-unit patch has 14 points at the median and 113 at most, the largest
  whole exterior grid 246. A search finishes in one frame with a budget of
  about 64 points, or over two or three frames for the biggest grids.
- **B, Quake.** Works everywhere, but cannot get around a building to a
  player out of sight. `path_corner` chains are what the opening already does
  with the original travel targets; for town wander they would duplicate what
  the grid already says.
- **C, navmesh.** The best paths, but the bytes, the builder dependency and
  the run-time polygon queries all go the wrong way on a 68040; the gain over
  D is smoother paths in open terrain, where B already works.
- **D, hybrid.** Each layer only runs when the cheaper one failed: the chase
  costs one step per think, the probe only runs when stuck, the search only
  when the target is out of sight or the probe failed.

Collision: all actors trace with the standing hull (hull 1, the player's
14.64-unit box; `tools/player_hull.py`), so a passage open to the player is
open to an NPC and vice versa, and the stair rule (risers up to 8.5 units
become walkable treads) applies to NPCs for free. The builder's graph gate
(CHIM page) walks every link with the same step on the same collision.

Cross-cell, cross-chunk and doors: grids are stitched by the builder where a
walk across the border passes (596 exterior border points face a gridded
cell); CHIM links address points in neighbouring sectors directly, so a path
crosses chunk and cell borders without a special case. Inside a cell, a door
is a flagged point: the NPC opens it (the door's collision changes) and goes
on. Load doors (to and from interiors) follow the
[traversal rules](NPC_CELL_TRAVERSAL.md): hostiles normally stop at the exit;
followers and escorts may pass. How companions are taken through a load door
(OpenMW's rules: a follow package aimed at the player, within 800 Morrowind
units, arriving at the door's destination) and how a follower survives an
AmiWind map change: [followers across doors and cells](chim/CHIM_NPC_PATHFINDING.md#followers-across-doors-and-cells).

## Recommendation and staged plan

Option D, built as a ladder that starts with what Quake already gives us
(owner direction, 9 October 2026). Every stage has a measurement before it is
called done, a regression test, and the full gate.

1. **Quake chase with stair climbing.** NPCs move with `AW_ActorStep` (the
   player's step: 8.5-unit step, 0.69 slope limit), driven by Quake's chase
   logic (`SV_StepDirection` / `SV_NewChaseDir` order of directions), from
   QuakeC through a new builtin (for example `aw_walkmove(yaw, dist)`), since
   `walkmove`/`movetogoal` use `SV_movestep`'s stricter corner test. Every
   flight that passes the stair gate is then climbable by NPCs too. Missing
   today: the builtin and its QuakeC declarations, walk poses for town
   residents (only the opening actors have them), more than one walker,
   `MOVETYPE_STEP`/ground flags for residents, and any hostile behaviour.
   Measure: an engine trace counter (traces, clipnode visits, cycles) per step
   on the cycle-exact preset at fixed Balmora and Seyda Neen poses; links of
   the original grids walked by the engine (the probe above predicts 97 % and
   92 %).
2. **Stuck check and local probe.** Stuck = less than half the expected
   progress over about 1.5 s (as OpenMW). Then (a) a fan of 8-16 short hull
   traces around the NPC, picking the open direction that best reduces the
   distance to the target or opens a line of sight (a wider `SV_NewChaseDir`);
   (b) if that fails, a tiny flood fill (about 8 x 8 step-sized cells,
   classified by traces, breadth-first to the cell nearest the target). Only
   when stuck, only near the player, spread over one or two frames; needs no
   grid. Cost estimate: a 16-way fan 1.7 ms (median) to 15 ms (mean) in
   Balmora, 5-52 ms in Seyda Neen; the flood fill is about twelve fans, so it
   stays rare (at most one per NPC every few seconds). Measure: cycles per
   probe and probes per second on the cycle-exact preset.
3. **Grid routing on a local patch.** When the target is out of sight or the
   NPC is stuck or kited: an A\* on the stored walkable graph around NPC and
   target (a few hundred units), the target's position as goal, a fixed node
   budget per frame; follow the route until the target is in sight, then back
   to stage 1. If the patch has no path (the target on a rock or ledge), the
   NPC switches behaviour (ranged attack, back off, wait at a sensible spot)
   instead of grinding into a wall. The graph comes from the path grids where
   they exist and from the builder's own generator elsewhere. Measure: A\*
   expansions and cycles per search, resident bytes, gate results.
4. **Packages and borders.** Wander inside the authored distance on grid
   points, travel, escort and follow; doors; actors handed across chunk and
   cell borders by the traversal rules. Measure: actors moving at once at
   fixed poses, frame time before and after.

Stage 1 and 2 need no new data and can start with the first combat work;
stage 3 needs the CHIM graph format and gate.
